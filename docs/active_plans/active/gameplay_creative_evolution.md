# Gameplay creative evolution

**Status: COMPLETE (2026-10-23).** The selected direction is implemented, and the final stage-24
payoff reproduces on the refined build. Fresh organic-recovery captures, source/spec review, visual
review, and final integration assessment accept the bounded illustration change and gameplay arc.

## Aim

Create fun-first, distinctive, memorable stages that make the medical setting part of what players
do. Build on dependable existing controls and recognizable shelter carving, while honoring the
rule that a break never triggers a merge. Seek authored combinations and interactions that give
stages their own character, and make successful treatment visibly restore the world.

## Exploration

- Start from the player's moment-to-moment choices and stage identity, then find the clearest
  medical action that can shape those choices and their consequences.
- Try small playable variations with existing controls and mechanics before adding systems. Explore
  combinations, authored objectives, healing with tactical consequences, mastery carried between
  stages, and organic carving as possible directions; none is required or rejected in advance.
- The selected arc uses three different forms of agency: display 12 (stable ID 20) earns the
  left current at x=320 after the first clear; the manual right switch at x=640 overrides it for
  four seconds, then the earned left baseline resumes during cooldown. Display 16 (stable ID 22)
  uses the center switch at x=480 to gather the right current at x=780, enabling a safe crossing
  left to the shield at x=132. Display 24 (stable ID 15) earns the right current at x=640 after
  its first clear; an optional left-current override at x=320 offers player-directed positioning
  and crossing before the right baseline resumes. The recorded saved-fan route uses a no-fire flank;
  it does not establish a shield-crossing benefit. The optional display-23 seam
  (stable ID 24) was tested and removed; the encounter's original right shield, shift, and steady
  gather remain untouched. Exact contracts and completed route evidence are in the ledger.
- Keep reliable movement and firing, useful shelter, legible hazards, and the no-merge-on-break
  rule intact. A promising concept should clarify or enrich play without making the controls fickle.
- Give the strongest ideas a visible payoff: the patient, artery, or surrounding tissue looks and
  behaves more restored because of what the player did.
- Use focused actual-control play and captures to compare candidate ideas. Record what was tried,
  what made play more distinctive or enjoyable, what confused or weakened it, and what remains
  unknown. Expand only the strongest direction, then repeat the cycle as needed.

## Working method

Make the next useful playable experiment directly from the current evidence. Keep the scope
independently executable and adapt the brief as findings accumulate. Focused behavior checks,
actual-control routes, and proportionate specification, quality, and integration review support
decisions; they are not a six-audit sequence or a human approval gate. Agent play and captures can
show behavior and presentation, but do not claim human enjoyment or usability evidence.

## Boundaries

Preserve the established left/right/fire interaction, shelter tradeoff, and split/merge contract
unless direct experiments support a deliberate, reviewed change. Preserve the game's medical
identity and existing patient-recovery meaning. Keep experiments narrow enough to compare and
replace; update the game specification and design decisions when a direction is selected.
